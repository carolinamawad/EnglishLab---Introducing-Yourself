Understanding 'To Be' in Context
CORE GRAMMAR
In English, to be serves the roles of both 
Spanish verbs: ser and estar.
We use it to express four essential categories 
of basic personal information:
• Identity (Who): I am Carolina.
• Origin & Place (Where): I am from 
Colombia.
• Role & Profession (What): I am a student.
• Condition & Emotion (How): I am happy.

--- Page 11 ---
The Four Uses of 'To Be'
1. Identity (Who)
State your name and identity.
“I am Carlos.” / “She is Ana.”
2. Origin (Where)
Indicate where you come from.
“I am from Medellín.”
3. Profession (What)
Describe your job or occupation.
“He is an engineer.”
4. Feelings (How)
Describe emotional condition.
“We are excited!”

Forming the Negative: Add 'Not'
In English, to make a statement negative, we place the word not directly after the verb to 
be. The basic structure is: Subject + to be + not + complement.
In Spanish, you say "Yo no soy doctor." In English, never say "I no am doctor." The word not 
must always follow am, is, or are: "I am not a doctor."

--- Page 8 ---
Negative Forms and Contractions
KEY CONCEPT
I · First Person
Full form: I am not
Contraction: I'm not
Example: I'm not tired today.
You / We / They
Full form: are not
Contractions: aren't / you're not
Example: We aren't ready yet.
He / She / It
Full form: is not
Contractions: isn't / she's not
Example: She isn't in the office.
⚠
️
Mistake to avoid
There is no contraction like "amn't". 
The only correct short form is "I'm 
not".

The Indefinite Articles: A and An
Use 'A'
Use a before singular nouns that begin with a 
consonant sound.
Examples: a book, a desk, a student, a city.
Use 'An'
Use an before singular nouns that begin with 
a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u).
Examples: an apple, an engineer, an office, an 
actor.

the written letter.
The article rule in English depends completely on the first 
sound when spoken aloud, never the written spelling:
• Silent 'H': an hour (sounds like "our" with a vowel sound, 
so use an).
• Voiced 'H': a house, a hospital (the 'h' is pronounced as 
a consonant).
• 'U' sounding like 'Y': a university, a user (starts with the 
consonant sound /j/).
💡Tip
Say the noun out loud. If your vocal cords start with 
an open vowel sound, use an.

Both a and an mean one (singular). Because of 
this meaning, they can never be used with plural 
or uncountable items:
•
Plural nouns: Say "They are students" (never "a 
students").
•
Uncountable nouns: Say "I need water" or 
"some coffee" (never "a water").
•
Nationalities: Say "She is Colombian"

Numbers and Phone Numbers
Ages and Dates
Say "I am 24 years old." For birthdays, 
combine month and day: "My birthday is on

Phone Numbers
Pronounce phone numbers single digit by

--- Page 12 ---
Family and Possessives
When you present your classmate, speak clearly 
about their family members and belongings using 
possessive forms.
•
His: "His name is Mateo. His father is an 
engineer."
•
Her: "Her name is Valeria. Her mother is a doctor."
•
Their: "Their family lives in Medellín."
⚠
️
Common Mistake
Do not say "Her father is Pedro" if your 
partner is male! Use His for men and Her for 
women.

Capital Letters
In English, always capitalize countries and 
nationalities: Colombia, Colombian, 
Spanish.
Sentence Structures
Distinguish: "He is from Peru" (Country) vs. 
"He is Peruvian" (Nationality adjective).
